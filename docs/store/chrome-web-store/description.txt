/dev/null, right inside GitHub, and on any page you read.

/dev/null (dev-null.sh) is where developers share what they ship, in short commits. This extension brings it to GitHub: read what people say about an organization, and commit about the repository, issue or pull request you're looking at, without switching tabs. Anywhere else, share the article you're reading, with what you think of it.

It looks like GitHub, in its light, dark and high-contrast themes, and speaks like /dev/null.


WHAT IT ADDS TO GITHUB

▸ A /dev/null tab on organizations
Next to Repositories and People, the /dev/null tab shows the organization's log: every commit that mentions it, with its images, quotes, link previews, replies and stars. Write a commit right above the log, with Write and Preview tabs, as you would comment on an issue.

▸ A Commit button wherever there's something to share
On a repository, an issue, a pull request, a discussion or a release, a Commit button opens /dev/null's composer, ready to share the page:
• insert the page's link, the author's mention or the organization's mention from the toolbar; nothing is added unless you choose to;
• type @ to mention anyone on /dev/null: the organization and the author come first, then the people you follow, then everyone matching what you type;
• attach up to 3 images, each with a description for people who can't see it;
• preview the commit exactly as /dev/null will publish it.

▸ /dev/null next to discussions
An organization's discussions show the latest commits mentioning it, in an "On /dev/null" section next to GitHub's own.

▸ Your profile and settings in the toolbar
The extension's popup shows your /dev/null profile, and lets you turn off anything the extension adds, one by one.


AND ON ANY PAGE

▸ Commit the page you're reading
From the popup, "Commit this page" opens a composer on the page in your tab:
• its link is already there, the tracking parameters (utm_*, fbclid…) removed, and the card /dev/null will show is above the text: its image, title and description;
• text you selected on the page comes in as a quote;
• /dev/null is not a bookmark feed: Commit turns on once you've written 50 characters of your own, the link and the quote left out;
• a page that looks private (localhost, your network, a link with a token) gets a warning before you share it.

▸ Alt+Shift+D, from the keyboard
On any page, Alt+Shift+D opens the same composer over the page, without reaching for the mouse. On GitHub, it opens the page's own composer. Change the shortcut in your browser's settings, or turn it off in the extension's.

▸ Nothing written is lost
Close a composer, or reload the page, and what you wrote comes back when you return, images included, as on dev-null.sh: stash@{0}, with a way to drop it.


GETTING STARTED

1. Install the extension.
2. Click its icon in the toolbar, then "Join with GitHub".
3. Approve this browser on dev-null.sh, as one of your devices, as you would a phone.

That's it: open any organization on GitHub and click its /dev/null tab. Disconnecting from the popup revokes the device on /dev/null.


PRIVACY

• The extension talks to dev-null.sh, to read logs and publish your commits, and to api.github.com, to look up the owners of the pages you share. Nothing else.
• Outside GitHub, it reads a page only when you ask: when you open its popup or press Alt+Shift+D, and only that page. It reads its address, title, link card and the text you selected, to prepare the commit; the card's image loads from the site itself. Nothing leaves your browser until you commit.
• What you write stays in your browser until you commit or drop it, and is forgotten when you disconnect.
• No analytics, no tracking, no ads.
• Your key is generated in your browser and can't be read back, not even by the extension: it only signs your requests to /dev/null.
• It only uses /dev/null's public API and its existing sign-in.
